# Digital Platform Design (TU Delft MSc CoSEM, SEN1641)

Two reports from the course.

## `battcycle-individual-report.pdf` (individual)

A high-level design of BattCycle, a fictional digital platform for sharing and repurposing batteries in the EU. The report follows the platform design process: the circular-economy challenge and domain, platform objective and type, stakeholder and user analysis, architecture and core components, business model, and an evaluation with critical reflection.

## `data-spaces-group-report.pdf` (group of four)

A comparison of four European data spaces (BDI, EONA-X, DSTM and SCSN). The report states the problem (many data space projects do not scale beyond the pilot phase), sets research questions and a methodology, builds an analytical framework from the literature (key features and their trade-offs: governance, architecture, trust, openness), applies it to each data space and ends with recommendations.

## What I learned

- **A platform is more than software.** Governance, trust, incentives and the business model decide whether a platform survives, next to its technical architecture.
- **Designing for several stakeholders at once.** Participants on both sides of a platform want different things, and the design has to balance them.
- **From literature to framework to cases.** Reading papers to extract features and trade-offs, turning them into a framework and applying the same framework to several cases keeps a comparison fair.
- **Data sovereignty in practice.** Data spaces are an attempt to share data while each party keeps control of it, which links directly to my interest in standardised and secure data exchange.

The group report was written with three other students.
