Python for finance

Comillas University, Madrid · 5 practice sessions

What I built. NPV, IRR and payback as functions; bond pricing and yield to maturity; duration and convexity; a Nelson-Siegel-Svensson yield curve fitted on US Treasury data; and a maximum-Sharpe portfolio found with constrained optimisation.

What I learned
  • Writing NPV and IRR myself showed why they can rank projects differently.
  • A bond price and its yield are the same thing seen from two sides; the yield needs a numerical solver.
  • Portfolio risk comes from the covariance matrix, and portfolios can be compared by Sharpe ratio.
Useful for

Valuing cash flows and bonds, measuring interest-rate risk, and basic portfolio and risk analysis in Python.

  • pandas
  • scipy.optimize
  • numpy-financial

Predicting travel mode, with explanations

TU Delft MSc · group project of five · Mobility Panel Netherlands

What I built. Merged several SPSS survey files into one clean table, then compared multinomial logistic regression, a random forest, XGBoost and a soft-voting ensemble with cross-validation, and explained the model with SHAP values.

What I learned
  • Most of the work is data preparation, not model fitting.
  • Accuracy alone is misleading with unbalanced classes; class weights and per-class precision and recall change what "good" means.
  • A prediction needs an explanation: SHAP shows whether the model relies on sensible variables.
Useful for

Building and comparing classification models on messy data and explaining the output. The same workflow applies to problems such as fraud detection.

  • scikit-learn
  • XGBoost
  • SHAP
  • pandas

Data science and statistics

TU Delft BSc · two data science courses

What I built. Exploratory analysis in pandas, sampling and bootstrapping, hypothesis tests, linear regression solved by hand and with statsmodels (dummy variables, interaction terms, residual checks), text clustering with TF-IDF, and my own analysis of an obesity dataset.

What I learned
  • Look at the data before modelling: missing values, distributions and outliers.
  • A confidence interval or test result is easier to trust after seeing it come out of bootstrapping.
  • Check assumptions, not just the fit: residual plots and Q-Q plots, not only R-squared.
Useful for

Exploring a dataset, testing whether differences are real, and building and checking regression models for a business question.

  • pandas
  • statsmodels
  • scipy.stats
  • scikit-learn

Network accessibility in Helsinki

TU Delft MSc · Design in Networked Systems

What I built. Combined district and transport-network shapefiles with adjacency matrices, and drew the networks on real coordinates with node size proportional to betweenness centrality.

Network science

TU Delft MSc · Design in Networked Systems

What I built. Functions that generate random graphs, dynamic random graphs and scale-free graphs (preferential attachment), plus clustering, path length, diameter, bridges and degree distributions.

What I learned
  • How a network is generated shapes its structure: random graphs have a bell-shaped degree distribution, scale-free graphs have a few hubs.
  • Different measures answer different questions: clustering, how far information travels, where a network is fragile.
Useful for

Describing and comparing networks of systems, actors or transactions with the right measures.

  • networkx
  • matplotlib

Theses

MSc thesis – Privacy threats in MCP and A2A

TU Delft, Complex Systems Engineering and Management · 2026 · Read the thesis in the TU Delft repository

Research on privacy threats in the API protocols for AI agents (MCP and A2A), with financial fraud detection at banks as the use case. The core question: how can systems communicate securely and in a standardised way on top of existing APIs? The work modelled the workflow end to end, mapped the threats against an AI privacy framework and validated them in expert interviews. Followed by further exploration of agentic payments: how do you authorise and verify a payment that an agent initiates on behalf of a user?

BSc thesis – Enhancing Digital Identity: Aligning DigiD with the eIDAS Regulations and EU Data Act

TU Delft, Technology, Policy and Management · July 2024 · graded 8.3/10 · Read the report (PDF) · Notes

The EU issues regulations that are developed separately, and each country has to fit them together in a working digital and legal system. This project asks whether the EU Data Act can be implemented in the Netherlands without DigiD fully complying with the Dutch Wet Digitale Overheid, which builds on eIDAS. Using a literature review, expert interviews and insights from the Identity Week Europe conference, it concludes that DigiD must meet the WDO requirements for citizens to share their data securely under the Data Act, and that the slow rollout of ID documents with the required chips is a further delay.
